# pt-PT-PLA — Language and course rules

Course modelled on the official programme **Português Língua de Acolhimento (PLA)** for people settling in Portugal,
units 2–9 (unit 1, alphabet and phonetics, is left out: the app has no audio). The learner prepares for the PLA exam:
**practical vocabulary and ready-made phrases for real situations in Portugal matter most**; grammar is taught where the
unit needs it. Every topic has **30 exercises** and is complete only when all are answered correctly, so every item must be
fair, unambiguous and solvable from the prompt.

## European Portuguese (same rules as pt-PT-A1)

- Informal singular is **tu** with 2nd-person verb forms (`tu falas`, `tu és`, `chamas-te`). Formal singular is **você / o senhor / a senhora** with 3rd-person forms. Plural: **vocês**. Never use `você` as the default informal form (that is Brazilian usage).
- Possessives take the **definite article** in European Portuguese: `a minha mãe`, `o meu pai`, `os teus irmãos`, `A minha casa é pequena.` Never write, and never accept as correct, an article-less possessive before a noun (`minha mãe`, `meu pai` — Brazilian usage), including in `answer`, `acceptedAnswers` and tile banks. (Vocative/fixed uses such as `Meu Deus!` are the only exception; avoid them at A1.)
- Progressive: **estar a + infinitivo** (`estou a falar`). Never the gerund (`estou falando`).
- Clitic pronouns after the verb in affirmative main clauses: **Chamo-me Ana**, never `Me chamo Ana`. After question words and negation they go before: `Como te chamas?`, `Não me lembro`.
- Vocabulary: comboio (not trem), autocarro (not ônibus), pequeno-almoço (not café da manhã), telemóvel (not celular), casa de banho (not banheiro), sumo (not suco), rapariga (girl), miúdo/a, fixe (cool, informal), giro/a (cute/nice), talho, bica (espresso, Lisbon), passadeira.
- Spelling per Acordo Ortográfico 1990 as used in Portugal: `ação`, `ótimo`, `direto`, but keep `facto` (PT) not `fato`.
- Numbers: **catorze** and **dezasseis, dezassete, dezanove** (PT), not `quatorze`, `dezesseis`.
- Greetings: `Bom dia` (until lunch), `Boa tarde`, `Boa noite`. `Olá`, `Tudo bem?`, `Até logo`, `Até amanhã`, `Adeus`.
- `Obrigado` if the speaker is male, `Obrigada` if female. Exercises must not force one; accept both where relevant.
- Questions: `Como te chamas?` / `Como se chama?`; the emphatic `é que` is very common: `Onde é que moras?`, `De onde é que és?`. Use both plain and `é que` forms in content.
- Questions keep European order: question word (+ é que) + verb (+ subject): `Onde moras?`, `Onde é que moras?`, `A que horas é que jantas?`. Never `Onde você mora?` / `Que horas você janta?`.
- Times of day: `de manhã`, `à tarde`, `à noite` and `de noite` are all correct European Portuguese (`de noite` = at night / by night, `às dez da noite` = at 10 p.m.). Do not teach any of them as wrong.
- `sempre` normally follows the verb: `Como sempre pão ao pequeno-almoço`, `Levanto-me sempre cedo`. `Nunca` goes before it: `Nunca janto tarde`.
- Contractions are preposition + article: `a + o = ao`, `a + a = à`, `a + os = aos`, `de + o = do`, `em + o = no`, `por + o = pelo`. `Vou ao café` = `ir a` + `o café`.
- Keep sentences short and natural, the way people actually speak in Portugal.

## Content of this course

- **Real Portugal.** Use real institutions, documents and places: Finanças, Segurança Social, Loja do Cidadão, Centro de
  Saúde, SNS 24, Junta de Freguesia, AIMA (residence), CTT (post), Multibanco, Carris/Metro de Lisboa, CP (comboios),
  Continente / Pingo Doce. Documents: NIF, NISS, número de utente, cartão de residência, comprovativo de morada, passaporte,
  contrato de arrendamento, recibo de vencimento. Do not invent procedures; keep them simple and true.
- **People.** Vary names, including the names learners of PLA meet in class: Sunita, Raj, Fátima, Oksana, Ahmed, Li, Mariana,
  João, Ana, Pedro, Inês. Nationalities in both genders (indiano/indiana, paquistanês/paquistanesa, nepalês/nepalesa,
  ucraniano/ucraniana, brasileiro/brasileira, chinês/chinesa, marroquino/marroquina, cabo-verdiano/cabo-verdiana).
- **Ready-made phrases.** Phrases a learner needs in a situation (Queria marcar uma consulta, Preciso de um comprovativo de
  morada, Tenho uma marcação às dez, Podia repetir, por favor?) are taught as whole chunks. If a chunk contains grammar not
  yet taught (queria/podia/gostava/preferia, Não se esqueça, Faça favor), the explanation says to learn it as a fixed phrase.
- **Register.** Staff, officials, doctors, employers: o senhor / a senhora (3rd person). Friends, colleagues of the same age,
  family: tu.
- **Prompts are self-contained.** In build_phrase and question_for_answer the English prompt contains every content word,
  the subject/person, the tense and every detail of the answer, so a learner typing from the prompt alone can produce it;
  for question_for_answer it says exactly what to ask and to whom (formal or informal).

## Exercise mix for this course (overrides the general mix; 30 exercises per topic)

- **Vocabulary / situation topics** (most topics): 5 translate_word (toTarget: English → Portuguese, allow letters/typing),
  2 match_pairs, 6 fill_gaps, 7 build_phrase, 6 question_for_answer, 3 find_error, 1 about_me. Difficulty ≈ 8 / 14 / 8.
- **Grammar topics**: 8 build_phrase, 7 question_for_answer, 6 fill_gaps, 4 find_error, 3 translate_word, 2 about_me.
  Difficulty ≈ 6 / 14 / 10.
- order_dialogue may replace one match_pairs in situation topics (service desk, pharmacy, interview).
