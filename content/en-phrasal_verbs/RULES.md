# Course rules — English Phrasal Verbs (B2)

- Write all learner-facing content in clear, natural English: prompts, explanations, examples, answer choices, and dialogue lines.
- Teach common contemporary meanings and uses suitable for B2 learners. Follow the meanings, register labels, grammar notes, and examples in the topic brief; do not invent meanings or silently change a phrasal verb's pattern.
- The topic's learner-facing description/context must cover every phrasal verb in its coverage list that is practised by its exercises. For each one, give a short meaning/use description and a natural example sentence. Do not leave any practised target out, even if it has a synonym.
- When the source brief says two phrasal verbs are synonymous or interchangeable for the practised meaning (for example, "doze off" and "nod off"), accept both as correct wherever either one answers the exercise. Put valid alternatives in `acceptedAnswers` or make the options unambiguously accept both; never mark a valid synonym as a distractor.
- Do not use `about_me` in this course. Every exercise must have an objectively gradable answer that tests recall or use of its assigned phrasal verb.
- Keep explanations short and useful. State the meaning or typical use being practised and include a natural example sentence when the exercise format allows it.
- Use phrasal verbs naturally in varied contexts. Across each topic, vary tenses, forms, and sentence patterns where natural; do not force an unnatural tense or use.
- Make each exercise clearly test its assigned target phrasal verb. Follow the exercise-to-phrase coverage assignments in the prompt and include exactly the assigned `coverage:<item-id>` tag in that exercise's `tags`.
- Create at least the required number of distinct exercises for every listed phrasal verb. Do not count an exercise that merely mentions a phrase without testing its meaning or use.
- Vary exercise types and contexts to aid recall. Prefer a mix of recognition and production, including gap-fill or sentence-building tasks where suitable.
- When an exercise has distractors or answer options, prefer plausible phrasal verbs with similar or easily confused meanings. Make the context specific enough that only the intended answer is correct.
- Do not give away the answer before submission. Check every learner-visible stimulus field, including `prompt`, `question`, `givenAnswer`, `sentence`, `context`, and `template`: do not include the assigned phrasal verb in any tense or separable form, a listed interchangeable synonym, or the complete expected sentence. In `find_error` only, the deliberately incorrect phrase in `sentence` may contain the target verb with the wrong particle. Describe the situation or communicative goal instead. For example, ask what time teenagers should return home, rather than telling the learner to build "Teenagers shouldn't stay out late." In `question_for_answer`, make sure `givenAnswer` does not itself contain the target phrase; it should answer the question the learner is asked to form.
- For open typed answers, add natural, meaning-preserving variants to `acceptedAnswers` for `build_phrase`, `question_for_answer`, and `translate_word`. For `fill_gaps`, put valid word alternatives in the relevant gap's `accepted` list. Include listed interchangeable phrasal verbs and normal wording variants that the prompt does not rule out. If politeness is not explicitly required, do not require `please`; accept both polite and neutral versions. Keep the primary answer and tile choices unchanged when only adding typed-answer alternatives.
- Avoid obscure, dated, or highly specialised meanings unless the brief explicitly includes them. Preserve informal, offensive, or other register labels and avoid presenting such expressions as neutral.
- Keep sentences concise, grammatically correct, and realistic. Do not repeat the same example or merely change a name or tense to create a new exercise.
- Follow the shared exercise schema, pedagogy, and validation requirements exactly. Return only the requested JSON object, with no markdown fences or commentary.
